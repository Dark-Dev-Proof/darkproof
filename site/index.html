What it does

Storms, blackouts and earthquakes take down mobile networks first, and every app that depends on a server goes quiet with them. DarkProof needs neither.

(01)

Group chat that works offline

Your family or team shares one group. Messages are encrypted end to end, and every DarkProof phone nearby helps carry them, even phones that can't read them.

(02)

“I'm safe” in one tap

Check in as safe or ask for help. Your group sees who is fine, who needs help, their battery level and, if you choose, where they are.

(03)

Water, shelter, power

Share places where people can find water, shelter, charging or medical help. Everyone nearby sees them, nearest first, with a compass to get there.

(04)

Reaches further

Messages hop from phone to phone for up to 7 days. Wi-Fi routers and LoRa radios stretch the range, and any phone that gets internet passes them on worldwide.

(05)

Answers with no internet

An assistant that runs on the phone answers first aid and survival questions, once you have downloaded its model. It can be wrong, so check anything important.

How it works

Three steps, and the first two take a minute.

  1. 01

    Create a group

    DarkProof makes a secret code for your family, friends or building. There is nothing to sign up for.

  2. 02

    Invite your people

    They scan a QR code or open a link, and an admin of the group lets them in.

  3. 03

    Open it when the network is down

    Phones with DarkProof nearby link by themselves and carry your messages, one phone at a time.

Install it before you need it, and ask your family to do the same.

[ How it travels ]

Phone to phone when everything is down. Through sealed relays when there is internet.

When everything is down

No towers, no internet, no power. Phones with DarkProof find each other and carry the messages themselves.

The five things that make it work

  1. Phones link directly

    Over Bluetooth within about 10 to 50 m, and over Wi-Fi when phones share a network, even a router or hotspot with no internet. iPhone and Android link to each other.

  2. Every phone is a carrier

    A phone keeps the sealed messages it hears and passes them to the next phone it meets. Phones from other groups carry them too, without being able to read them.

  3. Hop by hop

    A message can be passed on up to 16 times and keeps travelling for 7 days. It arrives when a chain of phones, or one person walking across town, connects you.

  4. Further with a radio

    Pair a small Meshtastic or MeshCore LoRa radio and short messages reach other radios kilometers away.

  5. Nothing to switch on

    The app does all of this by itself. What you write with nobody in range waits on the phone and leaves when someone comes near.

When there is internet

Your phone still talks to the phones around it. It also hands the same sealed messages to public relays, so they reach people in another city or another country.

The five things that keep it private

  1. Sealed before it leaves

    Every group message is encrypted on your phone. A relay only ever holds sealed packets it can't open.

  2. Public Nostr relays, none of ours

    DarkProof uses relays of the open Nostr network, run by other people. There is no DarkProof server to shut down or to ask for your data.

  3. Through Tor

    The app connects through Tor unless you turn that off, with its own route for each group, so a relay doesn't learn your internet address.

  4. Kept for a week

    Packets stay on the relays for up to 7 days. A phone that was offline catches up when it gets a connection again.

  5. One phone can connect a street

    A phone that gets internet also passes on the sealed messages it carries for others, so it links the phones around it to the rest of the network.

What a relay can still see: about when a packet was sent and which of a few fixed sizes it has. Anyone can download the sealed packets, but only your group can read them.

[ Windows · early access test build ]

Download for Windows

Version 0.4.13, 152 MB. For Windows 10 and Windows 11.

  1. Download the file on your PC and open it.
  2. Windows shows a blue warning, because the installer is not signed. Click “More info”, then “Run anyway”.
  3. Follow the installer. It needs no administrator password. If you have the earlier test build, install this one over it.
  4. If Windows asks, allow DarkProof on private networks. Without that it can't find phones.

Today it has the same groups and chats as the phones, and links with them over the same Wi-Fi network. Not on the desktop yet: Bluetooth, internet relays and Tor, and the map.

It has an offline AI on the Ask page. You choose one of five models in the app and download it once, from 1.3 GB to 16.8 GB; each says how much memory it needs, from 4 GB to 32 GB. After that it works with no internet. They are uncensored versions of the open models Qwen 3.5 and Gemma 4, made by other people: they answer anything and they can be wrong, also about medicine doses.

New in this build: a “Think first” switch in the Ask page's menu. With it on, the AI reasons before it answers, which takes longer. And on a PC with a suitable graphics card, the app asks once whether the card should do the AI's work. If the card can't be used, it goes back to the processor by itself.

This build has so far only run on test machines, not on a real PC with a real phone. No AI model has been loaded on Windows yet, and nothing has run on a real graphics card. Tell us what breaks.

Its engine and its AI engine have started from the opened disk image on two test Macs. Nobody has yet seen its window, opened it with a double click or linked a phone, so the steps above are what a Mac normally asks, not what we have seen. Tell us what breaks.

Questions

Does it really work with no internet?

Yes. Phones with DarkProof link directly over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi and pass messages along from one phone to the next. With a LoRa radio paired, short messages reach other radios kilometers away.

How far and how fast do messages travel?

As far as there are phones to carry them. A message waits on each phone and moves on when another DarkProof phone comes in range, so it can take seconds or hours. Phones keep passing a message on for 7 days.

Who can read my messages?

Only the people in your group. Other phones carry your messages but can't open them. Messages you send to Everyone are public: anyone with DarkProof can read them.

Has DarkProof had a security audit?

Not yet. No outside expert has reviewed DarkProof so far. The code and the message format are public so that anyone can check them.

What do you know about me?

Nothing. We run no servers and receive no data. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your name and group codes stay on your phone.

Does it share where I am?

Only if you turn that on, and only with your group, or with everyone for a place you choose to share.

Do I need an account or a phone number?

No. You pick a name and create or join a group, and that is all.

What happens when there is internet?

Phones that have internet also pass messages through public relays, so they reach people far away. They connect through Tor unless you turn that off. Nothing goes through servers of ours, because we have none.

Does it work between iPhone and Android?

Yes. The two apps use the same message format and talk to each other.

What does it cost?

Nothing. DarkProof is free and has no ads.

Is the phone on this page the real app?

It has two modes. The demo is a simulation that runs in your browser, with made up people and scripted answers. The recordings show the real app running in the iPhone Simulator. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

When can I download it?

On Android, Windows and Mac you can download an early test build today, from the install section of this page. You can also build the app from GitHub, for Android or iPhone, with the steps there. The App Store and Google Play versions are coming soon.
